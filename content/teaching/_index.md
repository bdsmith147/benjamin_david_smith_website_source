phys111:
  description: "Despite being the center of some of the “hottest” and most disruptive advancements of the 21st century, quantum mechanics has the reputation of being sterile, esoteric, and incomprehensible for the average human being. In truth, however, many of the problems people perceive in learning quantum mechanics stem from the language used to describe it and the apparent lack of meaningful associations. This course represents an attempt to open these learning opportunities to a wider audience. It is based on the assumption that anyone with interest and dedication can appreciate and begin to learn quantum mechanics."
  outcomes:
    - "Describe and differentiate between scientific fact, law, hypothesis, and theory."
    - "Examine and appraise scientific data, claims, and concepts for making wise decisions affecting themselves and others."
    - "Explain foundational quantum principles such as discreteness, superposition, probability, and entanglement."
    - "Identify applications and operations of these principles in a variety of modern and future technologies."
    - "Calculate physical quantities that are relevant to these applications."
    - "Distinguish the operation and capabilities of classical and quantum computers."
    - "Assess the social and ethical implications of upcoming quantum technologies."
    - "Express and reflect on quantum mechanical ideas, applications, or open questions in writing and other formats."
