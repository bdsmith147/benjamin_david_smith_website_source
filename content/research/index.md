      Benjamin's doctoral work in the ultracold quantum gases group explored quantum nonlinearities in two settings. In warm rubidium vapor inside a microwave cavity, he demonstrated coherent microwave-to-optical conversion that is tunable across a 550 MHz range of optical frequencies, a step toward linking microwave quantum devices to optical networks, and the same system led to a vector magnetometer. With ultracold Bose-Einstein condensates, he contributed to fast, low-noise quantum memory for single-photon-level light and developed GPU-accelerated simulations of spin-orbit coupled condensates.
