## Current work: Air Force Research Laboratory

*NRC Research Fellow, 2026 – present · Mentor: Dr. Spencer Olson*

Benjamin's current research is experimental atom interferometry with Bose-Einstein condensates in fiber Fabry-Perot microcavities, enhancing interactions between light and quantum gases to build the next generation of quantum sensors. His National Research Council fellowship project is titled “Realizing and Probing Strongly Interacting 1D Quantum Gases on a Hybrid Atom Chip–Cavity Platform.”

## Compact cold-atom interferometers: Sandia National Laboratories

*Postdoctoral Research Associate, 2024 – 2026 · Supervisor: Dr. Jongmin Lee*

At Sandia, Benjamin worked on quantum-enhanced gravity and acceleration sensing using cold-atom matter-wave interferometry, with applications toward practical inertial guidance and navigation. The work focused on making atom interferometers small, fast, and sensitive, using a grating magneto-optical trap that runs at data rates up to 100 Hz.

- [A Compact, High-Data-Rate, High-Sensitivity GMOT-Based Light-Pulse Atom Interferometer]({{< relref "/publication/smith-2026-gmot-atom-interferometer" >}}) (preprint, 2026)
- [Cold atom systems for deployable quantum inertial navigation and gravitational sensing]({{< relref "/talk/2025-06-cold-atom-navigation-damop" >}}) (poster, DAMOP 2025)

## Light and atoms for quantum technologies: University of Alberta

*Ph.D. research, 2016 – 2024 · Supervisor: Professor Lindsay J. LeBlanc*

Benjamin's doctoral work in the ultracold quantum gases group explored quantum nonlinearities in two settings. In warm rubidium vapor inside a microwave cavity, he demonstrated coherent microwave-to-optical conversion that is tunable across a 550 MHz range of optical frequencies, a step toward linking microwave quantum devices to optical networks, and the same system led to a vector magnetometer. With ultracold Bose-Einstein condensates, he contributed to fast, low-noise quantum memory for single-photon-level light and developed GPU-accelerated simulations of spin-orbit coupled condensates.

- [Microwave-to-optical conversion in a room-temperature ⁸⁷Rb vapor]({{< relref "/publication/smith-2023-microwave-optical-conversion" >}}) (*Communications Physics*, 2023)
- [Microwave-optical double-resonance vector magnetometry with warm Rb atoms]({{< relref "/publication/babaei-2026-vector-magnetometry" >}}) (*Applied Physics Letters*, 2026)
- [Storing short single-photon-level optical pulses in Bose–Einstein condensates]({{< relref "/publication/saglamyurek-2021-bec-quantum-memory" >}}) (*New Journal of Physics*, 2021)
- [GPU-accelerated solutions of the nonlinear Schrödinger equation]({{< relref "/publication/smith-2022-gpu-spinor-becs" >}}) (*Computer Physics Communications*, 2022) and the [Spinor-GPE]({{< relref "/software/spinor-gpe" >}}) package
- [Nonlinear interactions using neutral atomic gases]({{< relref "/publication/smith-2024-phd-dissertation" >}}) (Ph.D. dissertation, 2024)

## Extreme-ultraviolet optics: Brigham Young University

*Undergraduate research, 2013 – 2015 · Supervisors: Professors R. Steven Turley, David D. Allred, and Branton J. Campbell*

As an undergraduate, Benjamin refurbished a discharge light source for the 30.4 nm helium emission line, recommissioned a vacuum-ultraviolet monochromator, and measured the extreme-ultraviolet reflectance of thin-film Y₂O₃ at the Advanced Light Source to extract its optical constants. Earlier, he calculated thermal position distributions of crystal ions in various point symmetries.

- [Y₂O₃ optical constants between 5 nm and 50 nm]({{< relref "/publication/muhlestein-2019-y2o3-optical-constants" >}}) (*Optics Express*, 2019)
- [Reflectance Measurements of Two Thin Film Materials in the UV]({{< relref "/publication/smith-2015-senior-thesis" >}}) (senior thesis, 2015)
